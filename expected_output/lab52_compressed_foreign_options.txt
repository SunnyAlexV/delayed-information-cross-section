C.  BOVESPA, AND THE THREE THINGS THAT COULD EXPLAIN IT
================================================================================================
Section 9.4 leaves this as a shrug. Three explanations are available
without new data and all three are tested here.

  (i) THE CLOCK. Bovespa closes at 21:00 UTC with the S&P, so its most
      informative peer reaches it a session late under the rule of
      Section 3. Entering the S&P same-day is INADMISSIBLE and is run
      only as a diagnostic: if the clock is the cause, breadth should
      recover when the rule is broken.

VERDICT
================================================================================================
  With the block the paper actually recommends, the split of Section 9.4
  holds: 0 of 8 cells significant where the options market
  is the asset's own against 13 of 24 where it is another index's, on
  mean increments of +0.0029 and +0.0153. Compression raises both, as
  Section 5 says it must, and the ordering is unchanged, so the
  comparison was not an artefact of making the cross-section pay for
  seven coefficients.

  The gradient behind the split also sharpens when the bill comes off.
  On the seven-regressor block it read -0.38; on the recommended block
  the closeness of the options market to the target correlates -0.78
  with what the cross-section still adds, across the eight. Eight points
  remain eight points and no interval is quoted, but the relationship is
  no longer something a reader has to squint at.

  And Bovespa is explained, by the plainest of the three candidates.
  It was paying for six coefficients it did not need: -0.0144 with seven
  regressors, +0.0098 with one. The target that looked as though it held
  its own options market was the target whose estimation bill was
  largest relative to what its cross-section carried. Nothing about
  Brazil, and nothing about options.

  The other two candidates are ruled out rather than left open.
  Breaking the admissibility rule in Bovespa's favour, entering the
  S&P same-day, leaves the increment negative at all three delays, so
  the clock is not the cause. And the partial correlation of the peer
  factor given VIX, which is the quantity theory would reach for
  first, correlates -0.15 with the increment and predicts nothing.

  That is worth stating in the paper as a warning rather than as a
  curiosity. A cross-target comparison of NET increments is partly a
  comparison of estimation costs, and on one target in eight that was
  enough to reverse the sign of the conclusion.
