====================================================================================================
B3.  WHY A REAL COLUMN CAN LOSE TO AN EMPTY ONE
====================================================================================================
The ridge standardises each column on the training window and then applies
those statistics to a test day that may lie far outside it.  A Gaussian
AR(1) never produces such a day; a variance premium in a crisis does.  The
table is the largest standardised value each column actually presents to the
model, computed by replaying the refit schedule.

 delta   max |z| VRPus   max |z| NOISE   max |z| VRPde   days |z|>6
     0            26.4             3.8            36.9          100
     3            32.4             3.7            37.1          105
     5            32.4             3.6            37.1          110
    13            33.5             3.9            41.6          126
    21            33.0             4.3            43.8          137
    55            28.1             4.3            43.8          167

So clip the tails - at the 1st and 99th percentile of the TRAILING
1250 days only, so nothing leaks forward - and run the same race.
If the deficit disappears, the premium's problem was its tails in a ridge,
not its content.  If it survives, the tails were never the explanation.

VERDICT
====================================================================================================
  beats an uninformative column of the same width: domestic premium at 0/6 delays, Germany's at 0/6; after clipping, 0/6 and 0/6.
  the stale realised leg costs something at 0/6; Germany's premium beats the domestic one at 0/6.
  Section 6's choice of the LEVEL over the PREMIUM cost it nothing.  The
  premium is not a stronger competitor on this sample, clipped or raw, so
  the objection that Section 6 raced the wrong horse does not survive.
  That is a defence of a section this project had already conceded, and
  it is the useful result here.
  The realised leg carries nothing this model can use, so the delay has
  nothing to take from it.  The premium's information, such as it is,
  lives in the implied leg - which Section 6 already had.
  Method note, and the part worth keeping: a raw variance premium loses
  to an empty column in a ridge that standardises on a training window.
  Clipping its tails causally repairs that.  Anyone regressing on a VRP
  this way is measuring their estimator's tail sensitivity, not the VRP.
