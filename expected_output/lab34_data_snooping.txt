target SPX, 4862 test days, 7 foreign peers
stationary bootstrap, mean block 10 days, 2000 draws

================================================================================================
0.  DOES THE TEST REJECT AT THE RATE IT PROMISES?
================================================================================================
Under a true null - every model a pure noise forecast of the same target -
a 5% test should reject about 5% of the time, and the Reality Check should
reject LESS often than SPA because it recentres hopeless models too.

  200 replications, 10 null models, 800 days each
  Reality Check rejects at 5%: 5.5%
  SPA rejects at 5%:           7.0%

  Sizes are usable.
  SPA rejects more often than the Reality Check, as Hansen predicts.

B.  WHAT DOES THE CORRECTION ACTUALLY COST?
================================================================================================
The uncorrected p-value for the paper's own model, against the corrected one
for the best of nine.  The gap is the price of the search.

  bootstrap resolution is 1/2000 = 0.0005; a p-value at or below it
  is reported as such rather than as a number the bootstrap cannot support.

  Where both columns sit below the bootstrap's resolution the honest
  statement is that the correction demonstrably costs nothing, not that it
  costs some particular amount: a ratio of two numbers the bootstrap cannot
  resolve measures the bootstrap, not the search.  An earlier version of this
  file printed that ratio and one row read 65,913, which is a statement about
  2,000 draws and nothing else.
