================================================================================================
VERDICT
================================================================================================
  Across eight domestic controls the substitution rate at eleven weeks
  runs from 60.7% to 74.3%, a spread of 14% points. The paper's control
  gives 71.5% and the strongest control by own-only skill, +MEAS, gives
  67.4%, a paired difference of -4.1% [-10.6%, -0.3%]. That reproduces Section S4
  from a second direction, on a different feature set and a different
  script, which is worth more than the agreement of a number with itself.

  The lowest figure in the table is 60.7%, from ALL, and it should not
  be quoted as the conservative rate. Look at what produces it: that arm
  scores 0.5749 own-only at the origin and -0.0644 at eleven weeks, so it is the
  strongest control in the set where the denominator is set and one of the
  weakest where the numerator is. R(delta) reads the curve at two points,
  and an arm that is strong at one and weak at the other moves the rate
  without being a better control anywhere. Reporting it as the headline
  would be the mirror of the error two referees warned about, made in the
  paper's own favour instead of against it.

  What the envelope does retire is the open-ended version of the
  objection. The control is no longer one choice a reader has to take on
  trust: eight are priced, the selection rule is stated before the
  numbers, and a ninth moves the headline only by beating all eight on
  own-only skill, under the same rule.

  Section 8 is unaffected. Under the stronger control the foreign block
  still adds nothing detectable given implied volatility at any delay,
  so the redundancy result was not resting on a weak domestic model
  either.
