    coordinate, which is not a puzzle: see the note below.

    Why the seasonal barely matters here, structurally rather than
    empirically: the target is a 12-month rolling variance, and a
    12-month window necessarily spans every calendar month, so a
    fixed month-of-year effect largely cancels inside the target
    before any model sees it. There is little seasonal channel to
    close on this coordinate, which is also why the Part B placebo
    came out negative rather than merely small.

==================================================================================================
VERDICT
==================================================================================================
  The seasonal objection was worth raising: month-of-year explains
  19.8% of monthly return variance on average and the twelve-month
  profiles correlate +0.89 across metros, so a fresh peer really
  could have substituted for a stale mark by revealing the calendar.

  return: a peer carrying ONLY the seasonal earns 4.2% where the
  real peer mean earns 72.9%.
  volatility: a peer carrying ONLY the seasonal earns -2.8% where the
  real peer mean earns 18.4%.

  So the rate is not a calendar effect on either coordinate. That is
  established by a placebo that could have convicted the design and did
  not, rather than by a control that could only have failed to change
  the answer.

  return: dummies 67.9%, causally deseasonalised 70.9%, untreated 72.9%.
  volatility: dummies 18.8%, causally deseasonalised 15.9%, untreated 18.4%.

  return: widening from 13 to 19 peers, six of them ragged, moves the
  rate from 67.9% to 67.5%.
  volatility: widening from 13 to 19 peers, six of them ragged, moves the
  rate from 18.8% to 19.7%.

  What the paper should take from this file. The seasonal objection is
  the first thing a referee will raise about an unadjusted monthly
  series, and the answer is not 'we included dummies'. It is that a
  synthetic peer built to carry the seasonal and nothing else cannot
  reproduce the result: that is Part B, and it is the sentence to put
  in the paper.

  Part C is a weaker claim and should be stated as the weaker one it is.
  On the return target the untreated arm sits +5.1% above the
  published treatment, so removing the calendar cost the paper points
  and the published figure is the conservative one.
  On the volatility target the untreated arm sits -0.4% from the
  published treatment, which is inside the noise: seasonal handling
  neither helps nor hurts there, for the structural reason printed in
  Part C, and no conservatism should be claimed from it.
