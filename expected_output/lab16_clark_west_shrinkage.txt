A.  IS THE PRECONDITION PRESENT?  PENALTY SELECTION, PER ARM
====================================================================================
The bias is proportional to the DIFFERENCE between the two arms'
two arms' shrinkage.  If both arms landed on the same penalty at every
refit, there would be nothing to discuss.

The arms agree at 51% to 82% of refits, and the cross arm is
penalised harder at 10% to 43% of them - a minority at every
delay. What holds at all 6 delays is that its MEAN penalty is the
higher of the two (6 of 6), which is the configuration that would
bias the statistic upward - the direction our column runs.

====================================================================================
B.  THE COMPANION PAPER'S DIAGNOSTIC:  mean adjustment / |mean differential|
====================================================================================
Near one, the adjustment matches the estimation penalty it removes.
Materially above one, it exceeds that penalty and the surplus is what
inflates the statistic.  We report it as a description, not a threshold:
there is no calibration here for how large it must be to matter.

VERDICT
====================================================================================
  diagnostic above 1.5 at 1 of 6 delays: 3

  What this licenses depends on the diagnostic above, and the paper should say
  only what it shows.  Where the ratio sits near one the Clark-West adjustment
  is matching the penalty it removes and the statistic is doing its job.  Where
  it is well above one the statistic is carrying a surplus, and the honest move
  is to report the Clark-West column with that ratio beside it rather than to
  quote a p-value that this mechanism can inflate.

  Either way the primary test is Giacomini-White, for the reason in part D, and
  nothing here touches the substitution rates, the intervals or the bootstraps -
  none of which involve a nested-model correction.
