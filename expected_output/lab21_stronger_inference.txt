A.  DOES THE COST OF BREADTH SURVIVE AN UNFITTED NULL?
========================================================================================
AR(1)   fitted Gaussian surrogates, matched on each peer's persistence and
        on the innovation covariance - lab07's generator, and the one the
        referee objects to because it cannot carry tails or clustering.
SHIFT   the real foreign block moved circularly, with the real missing-data
        mask held fixed.  Every marginal, tail and cluster is the real one;
        only the alignment is destroyed.  Nothing is fitted.
NAIVE   the same shift applied to the raw array, so the gaps move with the
        values.  Reported to show what that costs, not as a candidate.

  guard: 20 offsets over a 6427-row block, none closer than 200 rows to no shift at all
  trap 1: rolling the raw array instead would cost the surrogate arm 187 of 4862 test days per draw (3.8%),
          and the matching training rows, purely by moving the holidays.

========================================================================================
B.  AN INTERVAL FOR THE RATE THAT KNOWS ITS DENOMINATOR CAN VANISH
========================================================================================
R(delta) = [skill(cross,delta) - skill(own,delta)] / [skill(own,0) - skill(own,delta)].
Fieller inverts a t-test on num - R*den and solves the quadratic; where the
denominator is weak the confidence SET is two half-lines or the whole line,
which a percentile interval cannot report and so never does.

  correlation between the two differentials, measured, which is the parameter
  lab63 and lab66 set in their generator rather than estimating:
    d=1: 0.25   d=3: 0.60   d=5: 0.74   d=13: 0.86   d=21: 0.89   d=55: 0.89
    mean over the grid: 0.71, at delta = 55: 0.89

  confidence sets that are NOT an ordinary interval: 0 of 6 
  largest disagreement between Fieller and the percentile bootstrap, either endpoint: 8.2%

  Every row is an ordinary interval: no delay produced the two-half-lines or
  whole-line confidence set that weak identification would have shown.  But the
  two constructions are NOT interchangeable: the largest endpoint disagreement is
  8.2%, so the referee's objection is methodologically right and the choice of
  construction is not inert.  The denominator is weak at delta = 1 but not weak
  enough to break identification, and the published sets are the inverted ones.

  Two caveats keep this from being a clean vindication.  The first version of this
  function used iid variances and came out NARROWER than the block bootstrap at
  every delay, which is impossible for data whose targets overlap by five days and
  was the signal that it was wrong; the numbers above use HAC variances with the
  same Bartlett kernel as Section 6.  And delta = 0 is absent because its
  denominator is identically zero by construction, which is the one case where the
  ratio genuinely does not exist rather than being merely hard to pin down.
