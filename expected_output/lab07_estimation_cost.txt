================================================================================
P2.  DOES SHRINKAGE CURE IT?
================================================================================
Both models are forced to the same ridge penalty, so the comparison is
like for like.  Estimation cost is an artefact of estimating; it must
vanish as the penalty rises.  A genuinely misleading signal would not.

delta = 0 is where the cost should be ALL there is.
delta = 21 is where the paper claims a real benefit.
Both are shown, because the test only means something if they differ.

   lambda    dR2 at d=0   dR2 at d=21   reading
        1       -0.0102       +0.2586   cost present
       10       -0.0099       +0.2592   cost present
      100       -0.0078       +0.2638   cost shrinking
     1000       +0.0063       +0.2714   cost gone - sign flipped

The delta = 0 column must go to zero; the delta = 21 column must not.

================================================================================
P3.  IS THE COST THE SAME AT EVERY DELAY?
================================================================================
P1 measured the cost at delta = 0.  Treating it as a constant would be
convenient, and lab06's placebo says not to: shifted peers cost -0.019
at delta = 5 but -0.044 at delta = 21.

But a CIRCULAR SHIFT is the wrong instrument for this.  Shifting by two
or five years can park a crisis period against a calm one, and the
validation step will happily fit that spurious structure.  What it
measures is estimation cost PLUS misalignment damage, and the two are
not separable.  A first run confirmed the problem: four shift lengths
gave -0.0074 to -0.0424 for the same quantity at delta = 0, against a
tight -0.0100 from P1.  A statistic whose value depends that much on an
arbitrary choice of shift is not measuring what it claims to.

So build surrogates that are uninformative BY CONSTRUCTION: fit an
AR(1) to each peer and simulate from it.  Same persistence, same
variance, no relationship to the target at any lag.  Ten draws.

At delta = 0: surrogates give -0.0131 [-0.0137,-0.0126];
the peer-count line in P1 gives -0.0100.  They differ by 0.0031.
Read that as agreement on the MAGNITUDE, not as a formal match.  The
interval above is over surrogate draws only: it conditions on this one
sample of the target, so it understates the true uncertainty, and P1's
slope carries its own error that is not quantified here.  What the two
methods establish together is that the cost is about one R-squared
point at zero delay, which is what the argument below needs; neither is
precise enough to support a third decimal place.

Cost at delta 21 minus cost at delta 0: -0.0404.  It grows with delay.
That is not an artefact: as own information goes stale the fit weakens,
validation picks a looser penalty, and useless regressors get more rope.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS BUYS THE PAPER
================================================================================
Gross benefit = measured dR2 minus the cost of carrying the regressors
at that same delay.  Gross is what the foreign markets actually KNOW;
net is what a forecaster actually GETS after paying to estimate it.

Gross is a DIFFERENCE OF TWO ESTIMATED QUANTITIES, so it gets an
interval before it gets a label.  Two warning signs said this matters:
under heavy shrinkage in P2 the delta = 0 gap read +0.0063, four times
the +0.0016 the point estimate gives, and both are far smaller than the
interval lab06 put on the NET figure alone.

Each bootstrap replicate resamples test days in moving blocks of ten and
recomputes the net difference AND the surrogate cost on those SAME days.
Sharing the resample is what keeps the two errors correlated instead of
adding them blindly, and it is why no model is refitted here: both are
sums of per-day squared-error differentials cached above.

Gross content first clears zero at delta = 1.
A label is only claimed where the interval supports it.

Three things follow, and none of them were in the paper:
  1. At delta = 0 gross is +0.0028, interval [-0.0060,+0.0115],
     which covers zero and caps the content at about +0.011.  Foreign
     markets do not MISLEAD about the current US state; whatever they
     know about it beyond current domestic data is too small to measure
     here.  The negative was the bill, not the information.  That cap is
     the useful part against Korkusuz and Jayawardena et al.: it does not
     say they are wrong, it says how large their effect can be and still
     sit inside what we see.
  2. The tempting middle band - real signal that cannot yet pay for its
     own estimation - is NOT established.  At delta = 1 the interval is
     [+0.0009,+0.0280], which includes zero.  Suggestive, and not
     something the paper may assert.
  3. At delta = 21 estimation eats 17% of what the
     cross-section knows (+0.3141 gross -> +0.2605 net).  A method that
     estimated those seven coefficients more cheaply would recover part
     of it.  We have not built one and claim nothing about how much.
