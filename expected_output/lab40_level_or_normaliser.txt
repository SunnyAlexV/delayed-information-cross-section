The discriminating quantity is not the significance count, which is the
same for all three targets and is a statement about power. It is how much of
the gain SURVIVES re-dating the normaliser: a gain that is the model
predicting log M at t+5 must shrink when the target's normaliser is instead
one the model has already been handed.

================================================================================================
VERDICT
================================================================================================
  Re-dating the normaliser changes nothing: the level is worth the same
  against a denominator the model must predict and against one it has
  been given exactly. It cannot be buying that by predicting a quantity
  it already knows, so the gain is information about volatility. Knowing
  the absolute level of recent variance - not merely where it sits
  against its own median - is worth real skill to a stale forecaster,
  and the normalisation this literature applies without comment throws
  it away.
  Two qualifications belong with that. The gain reaches significance
  only at delta = 55 on every target ([55]), so the short-delay
  column is a direction rather than a result; and the rate correction in
  part B holds on the implementable target too, so Section 9.1's
  arithmetic stands even though its caveat does not apply.

  largest move in R(delta) from adding the level, on target C: 4.1%
