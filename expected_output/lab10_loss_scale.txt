====================================================================================
4.  WHAT THE NORMALISATION WAS DOING TO PATTON'S MSE
====================================================================================
This part exists because the paper described its MSE column as Patton's
MSE on the variance scale and computed it on the MEDIAN-NORMALISED
variance scale.  Two things separate those two columns, not one, and
reporting a single gap would credit the whole of it to the weight:

  MSE(nrm)   (exp y - g exp f)^2                 no median on either side
  MSE(inf)   (exp y * Mh - g exp f * Mh)^2       BOTH sides at t+h.  Per
             day this is exactly Mh^2 * MSE(nrm), so what separates it
             from the column above is the 1/M^2 weight ALONE.  It is
             infeasible: M_{t+h} is not known at t.
  MSE(nat)   (exp y * Mh - g exp f * M0)^2       the implementable one,
             which the paper reports.  What separates it from MSE(inf)
             is the median's h-day drift ALONE.

So gap 1 prices the weighting and gap 2 prices the drift, and the sum of
the two is the whole of the earlier mislabelling.

  identity check at delta = 55: max |MSE(inf) - M^2 MSE(nrm)| / M^2 MSE(nrm) = 1.59e-12
  -> the middle column IS the normalised loss reweighted: True

====================================================================================
5.  WHICH BENCHMARK BELONGS ON THE VARIANCE SCALE
====================================================================================
The benchmark forecasts the NORMALISED log target, y = log(var / M),
and is carried onto the variance scale exactly as the models are:
exponentiate, apply Duan's smearing factor, multiply by the median the
forecaster holds.  For a constant log forecast the smeared value is
mean_u exp(y_u), and an earlier version of this project called that
'the trailing mean of the variance'.  It is not.  It is the trailing
mean of the variance OVER ITS OWN MEDIAN, and the two are different
objects.  This part measures how different, and what turns on it.

  mean |M_t mean_u exp(y_u)  -  mean_u sigma^2_{u+h}| as a share of the
  mean natural variance: 62.8%; correlation between them: 0.35
  So the collapse an earlier draft claimed does not hold, and the
  reconstructed benchmark has to be described as what it is.

  same forecaster the log and QLIKE columns use and keeps Section 4's
  claim that the three columns share one benchmark.  The direct one is
  a different forecaster and is shown so the choice is visible.
