Prints the anatomy of the conditional rate, Section S5.

target SPX, 4862 test days, 7 foreign peers
    VIX files: ['VIX_History.csv']
VIX terciles at t-1: calm <= 15.4, stressed > 20.7
The VALUE is at t-1 and the BOUNDARIES are percentiles of the whole
test window, so a day's bucket is decided with the rest of the sample
in hand.  That is deliberate - a fixed boundary keeps 'stressed'
meaning the same thing in 2008 and in 2017, which a trailing boundary
does not - but it is NOT a rule a forecaster could have applied on the
day, and the paper called the cut a rolling one until this arm
existed.  Part E prices the difference instead of leaving the
word to carry it.

A.  THE RATIO, TAKEN APART
================================================================================================
N is the mean squared error the cross-section REMOVES at that delay.
D is the mean squared error the delay ADDS. R = N / D. Both are in the
target's own units, so they can be compared across states directly.

================================================================================================
B.  A CONFIDENCE SET THAT CAN SAY 'NOT IDENTIFIED'
================================================================================================
Fieller's construction on the same N and D, HAC-adjusted, from Appendix C.
'interval' is an ordinary two-sided set; 'exclusion' is the complement of
one, two half-lines; 'unbounded' excludes nothing at all. The last two are
answers a percentile bootstrap cannot give, and are the honest ones when a
denominator can approach zero.

================================================================================================
VERDICT
================================================================================================
  Stress multiplies the error the cross-section removes by 11.0, against
  6.3 for the error the delay adds. The numerator moves with the state,
  so the conditional result is a fact about markets and not an artefact of
  dividing by a larger denominator. Breadth does more work when markets
  are frightened, in absolute error and not merely as a share.

  Every calm-market set is an ordinary interval, so the percentile
  bootstrap was not misleading after all and the text's caveat can go.

================================================================================================
E.  THE SAME CUT A FORECASTER COULD HAVE MADE ON THE DAY
================================================================================================
Boundaries from each day's own trailing 252 sessions of VIX, taken at
t-1 with the value, instead of percentiles of the whole test window.
Same forecasts, same losses, same days: only which bucket a day falls
in changes. This is what the word 'rolling' in the paper has to mean
if it means anything, so the two cuts are printed side by side and
the paper quotes whichever it actually computed.
