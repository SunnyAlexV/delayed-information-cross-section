  Every target scored here sits in one cluster, between couplings of
  0.612 and 0.808, a band 0.196 wide. There is no
  weakly coupled target left to delete and refit without, because the two
  the earlier vintage had are the two whose exports cannot be obtained.
  So this is not the hardest version of the question any more; it is the
  only version the available data can be asked.

  What to take from this, and what not to:
  The correlation is not one point. The worst leave-one-out Pearson is
  +0.896 (dropping KLCI), the Spearman never falls below +0.900,
  and across the 6 the ordering holds at +0.943.

  The fitted LINE is a different matter. AXJO carries leverage
  0.685 against a mean of 0.333, and removing it moves the slope from
  +1.469 to +1.972 and the zero-crossing from +0.315 to +0.402.
  So the ordering is robust and the crossing is not. The paper should
  quote the correlation and treat the crossing as a location, not a
  threshold - which is what the text already says, now with the
  diagnostic that licenses saying it.

  6 points remain 6 points. None of this makes the relationship
  better estimated than a sample of 6 can make it; it establishes
  which of the two claims the paper makes that sample can carry.

================================================================================================
  worth less than nothing at eleven weeks: none
  repairs at least thirty per cent:        AXJO, HSI, N225, NSEI, JKSE, KLCI

  This is Section S27's mechanism read backwards. The recovery there is
  entirely the leading component of the foreign block, so a market that
  does not load on that component has nothing to recover with. Nothing
  here is special to these markets' options, and the paper does not claim
  it is.

================================================================================================
VERDICT
================================================================================================
  The question was whether Section S13's split survives where no
  volatility index exists to be held. On the 2 such targets that can
  be re-run it does. Indonesia repairs 42% and Malaysia repairs 44% of the delay damage
  with breadth, and a compressed cross-section still adds to implied
  volatility at eleven weeks with statistics of +2.05 and +1.51.
  There is no options market anywhere in that picture, so the reading of
  Section S13 does not depend on which volatility series we happened to
  buy.

  What this file no longer measures, and the paper no longer claims.
  CSEALL and KSE100 were scored in an earlier vintage of this
  study and were the two least coupled markets in the set. Their
  exports can no longer be obtained from the source that supplied them
  and are not held here, so any rate computed on them could be
  reported but never re-run, by a reader or by us. They are dropped on
  that ground and on no other: the rule is applied before any rate is
  read and would drop a target whichever way it came out. What goes
  with them is every observation of a WEAKLY coupled target, so the
  coupling statistic below ranks the markets that remain and cannot
  show what happens where coupling is small. That limit is the price
  of reporting only what can be re-run, and it is stated rather than
  worked around.

  The scope condition the paper keeps is the ordering: breadth
  substitutes for a stale mark further where the market is more coupled
  to the cross-section it is offered. Section 10 already refuses to
  extrapolate the rate to a private asset, on the grounds that nothing
  in the paper bounds that asset's correlation with the public
  cross-section. These targets are what the refusal looks like when it is
  measured instead of asserted, over the coupling range they span and
  no wider.

  What none of this reaches is still an illiquid asset. Every target here
  is a liquid equity index with a daily open, high, low and close, which
  is the property that made it usable at all. The gap Section 11 names is
  narrower and it is not closed.
