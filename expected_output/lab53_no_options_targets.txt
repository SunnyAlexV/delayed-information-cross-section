  Drop BOTH low-coupled targets and fit on the remaining 6, which is the
  hardest version of the question, because what is left is one cluster:
    Pearson +0.923, Spearman +0.943

  What to take from this, and what not to:
  The correlation is not one point. The worst leave-one-out Pearson is
  +0.981 (dropping AXJO), the Spearman never falls below +0.964,
  and inside the single cluster of 6 the ordering still holds at +0.943.

  The fitted LINE is a different matter. KSE100 carries leverage
  0.729 against a mean of 0.250, and removing it moves the slope from
  +1.175 to +1.448 and the zero-crossing from +0.225 to +0.309.
  So the ordering is robust and the crossing is not. The paper should
  quote the correlation and treat the crossing as a location, not a
  threshold - which is what the text already says, now with the
  diagnostic that licenses saying it.

  Eight points remain eight points. None of this makes the relationship
  better estimated than a sample of eight can make it; it establishes
  which of the two claims the paper makes that sample can carry.

================================================================================================
VERDICT
================================================================================================
  Two things come out of this and only one of them was the question.

  The question was whether Section S13's split survives where no volatility
  index exists to be held. On two of the four it does. Indonesia and
  Malaysia repair 42% and 44% of the delay damage with breadth, and a
  compressed cross-section still adds to implied volatility at eleven
  weeks with statistics of +2.05 and +1.51. There is no options market
  anywhere in that picture, so the reading of Section S13 does not depend
  on which volatility series we happened to buy.

  The other thing was not the question and matters more. Pakistan and Sri
  Lanka are not a weaker version of the same result, they are a different
  result: -31% and -12% at eleven weeks, a cross-section that makes a
  delayed forecaster worse. They are also the two least coupled markets in
  the set. A mean over these four would average a result with its
  opposite, so this file does not quote one as a headline.

  The paper gains a scope condition it could not have guessed: breadth
  substitutes for a stale mark where the market is coupled to the
  cross-section it is offered, and not otherwise. Section 10 already
  refuses to extrapolate the rate to a private asset, on the grounds that
  nothing in the paper bounds that asset's correlation with the public
  cross-section. These four targets are what the refusal looks like when
  it is measured instead of asserted.

  What none of this reaches is still an illiquid asset. Every target here
  is a liquid equity index with a daily open, high, low and close, which
  is the property that made it usable at all. The gap Section 11 names is
  narrower and it is not closed.
