A.  THE INDUCED PERSISTENCE, MEASURED RATHER THAN ASSERTED
==================================================================================================
  A three-month moving average of independent monthly innovations has a
  first-order autocorrelation of 2/3 by construction, and zero at lag 3.
  If the panel's monthly persistence were ENTIRELY the smoother, the
  monthly column below would sit near 0.667 and the quarterly column,
  which shares no transaction month between consecutive observations,
  would sit near zero. Neither is what the panel does.

  Read the three columns together. Monthly persistence averages 0.800, which is

B.  THE ANSWER: THE WHOLE DESIGN ON NON-OVERLAPPING QUARTERLY CHANGES
==================================================================================================
  Index read on one phase of the quarterly grid at a time. Consecutive
  changes share no transaction month, the stale own mark and the fresh
  peer block share none either, and the label shares none with the peer
  block. Every channel the objection names is closed by construction.
  All three phases are run, because picking one would leave 'why that
  phase?' unanswered and the three are not the same sample.

  prediction committed before this ran: positive, inside [45%, 92%], on at least 10 of 14 metros

  clock: train 40 quarters, validate 8, refit every 4, horizon 1
  delays in QUARTERS: [0, 1, 2]; delta = 1 is one quarterly appraisal
  cycle, the same object Section 9.2 reads at delta = 3 months

C.  SENSITIVITY: THE SAME MONTHLY DESIGN ON A DE-SMOOTHED PANEL
==================================================================================================
  Geltner's reverse filter r* = (r - rho r[-1]) / (1 - rho), with rho
  estimated on each metro's first 120 months and frozen, so no
  observation is filtered using its own future. Everything else is
  Section 9.2 unchanged: same estimator, same peer block, same delays.

  rho by metro, estimated once on the initial training block:
        Los Angeles   0.91        New York   0.84   San Francisco   0.82
         Washington   0.82          Boston   0.73          Denver   0.66
          San Diego   0.63         Chicago   0.51       Cleveland   0.50
           Portland   0.48       Charlotte   0.37       Las Vegas   0.28
              Tampa   0.22           Miami   0.17

  rho averages 0.567 across the fourteen.
  noise amplification 1/(1-rho): mean 3.5x, worst 10.9x (Los Angeles).
  That is the cost of the inversion and the reason this is a
  sensitivity rather than a second headline: a rate that falls here
  may be telling us about the housing market or about the amplifier.

  delta     S_own   S_cross  delay cost  breadth adds  metros +
      0    0.3212    0.4150           -             -         -
      1    0.3224    0.4117     -0.0012       +0.0893  13 of 14
      2    0.2420    0.3796      0.0792       +0.1376  14 of 14
      3    0.2220    0.3571      0.0992       +0.1352  14 of 14
      6    0.1487    0.3068      0.1725       +0.1581  14 of 14

  This part reports increments in R-squared and NOT a substitution
  rate, and the table says why. R(delta) divides what breadth adds by
  what delay cost, and de-smoothing has removed most of what delay
  cost: one month of staleness now costs -0.0012 of R-squared against
  the smoothed panel's much larger figure, so the denominator is at or
  near zero and the ratio is the pathology the paper's own Appendix
  describes rather than a measurement. Quoting a percentage here would
  be quoting the reciprocal of a rounding error.

  What the increment column does say is worth having on its own terms:
  at delta = 3 the peer block still adds +0.1352 of R-squared on a
  panel with the moving average inverted out of it, positive on 14 of 14
  metros. Breadth is not paid for by the smoother.

  And the denominator's collapse is itself the finding a reader should
  take from this part: smoothing is most of what makes a stale mark
  expensive. Remove it and the forecaster loses little by being late,
  which is the same statement as 'there is less for breadth to repair'.

==================================================================================================
VERDICT
==================================================================================================
  Non-overlapping quarterly, one quarter of relative staleness, the
  three phases averaged: 56.9% pooled (43.6% as a mean of
  per-metro ratios), phases spanning [49.1%, 63.5%], positive in
  35 of 42 metro-phases and on 12 of 14 metros by phase average.
  The widest per-phase bootstrap interval on the pooled figure is [19%, 81%].
  Section 9.2's monthly figure at the same economic delay is 67.8%
  [45%, 92%], positive on 14 of 14.

  The prediction was: positive, inside [45%, 92%], on at least 10 of 14.
  Outcome: MET (point inside the band, 12 of 14 metros positive).

  What that does and does not establish, stated carefully, and counted
  rather than asserted:

  Of the 3 phases, 1 of them exclude zero on the mean-of-ratios
  interval and 3 exclude zero on the pooled one. So the two
  estimators do not agree about what has been established. Under the
  estimator Section 9.2 uses, a quarter of the observations buys roughly
  double the interval and the quarterly sample cannot carry the claim on
  its own; under the pooled one it can. Neither quarterly figure is
  offered as a second headline, and the paper does not quote one.

  It DOES dispose of the objection it was built for. The moving-average
  explanation is not a claim that the rate is smaller than 67.8%; it is
  a claim that the rate is MANUFACTURED, and a manufactured rate must go
  to zero when no two quantities in the design share a transaction
  month. Instead the point estimate lands at 56.9%, inside the monthly
  interval, in all three phases, with the sign holding on 12 of 14 metros.
  The artefact hypothesis makes a sharp prediction and the data do not
  match it. That is what this file was built to test, and it is all it
  is offered as.

  The de-smoothed panel agrees from the other direction: with the
  moving average inverted out, breadth still adds +0.1352 of R-squared
  at delta = 3 on 14 of 14 metros, while the delay cost that R(delta)
  divides by has collapsed, which is why Part C reports increments and
  refuses to quote a rate.

  What none of this addresses is the second, larger caveat Section 9.2
  already carries: an index is not a book. Nobody holds a Case-Shiller
  index, and the overlap question is about construction, not about
  whether a real appraised portfolio behaves like a published average.
