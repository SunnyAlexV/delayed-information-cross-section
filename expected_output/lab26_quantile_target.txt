equal 1/2 + arcsin(rho)/pi exactly.  If the quadrature cannot reproduce the
formula the note already uses, it cannot be trusted at any other quantile.

============================================================================================
A.  THE BAYES CUT MOVES WITH THE QUANTILE
============================================================================================
c*(q) = z_q / rho.  The note's rule is a > 0, which is c* only at q = 0.5.
rho is the in-sample correlation between the delayed and future states,
reported here to show the size of the misspecification, not used as a
forecast - the rules below estimate their cut from training data alone.

============================================================================================
C.  THE TWO RULES OPTIMISE DIFFERENT THINGS, AND THE TABLE SHOWS IT
============================================================================================
The fitted cut maximises 0-1 accuracy; away from the median that means
almost never calling the event.  The note's a>0 rule calls it about half
the time, so it catches events at the cost of false alarms.  Which one is
'better' is a choice of objective, not a fact, and both are reported.

============================================================================================
D.  CAN THE DATA SEPARATE ANY OF IT?
============================================================================================
Two questions, both answered from the balanced-accuracy intervals in part B:
is a rule better than chance, and is one rule separable from another?
Intervals are block-bootstrapped, so they carry the 5-day overlap.

     q  positives  episodes ~  rules above chance  pairs separated
  0.50        365          36             9 of 15          0 of 15
  0.65        257          25            11 of 15          0 of 15
  0.75        209          20             7 of 15          1 of 15
  0.90        104          10             1 of 15          1 of 15

  'Rules above chance' counts (rule, delay) cells whose balanced-accuracy
  interval lies entirely above 0.5.  'Pairs separated' counts pairs of rules
  at the same delay whose intervals do not overlap - a conservative test, and
  a deliberately demanding one.

  Read together with part C: where rules are above chance but not separated
  from each other, the honest statement is that the tail target is predictable
  and that this sample cannot say which rule predicts it best.  Nine years
  does not contain enough independent volatility spikes to settle that, and
  no change of estimator fixes a shortage of events.
