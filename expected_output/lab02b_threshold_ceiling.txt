==============================================================================
THE CENTRING ASSUMPTION, AND WHAT THE BENCHMARK IS WITHOUT IT
==============================================================================
Equation (*) is an ORTHANT probability: it is the chance that two
MEAN-ZERO normals share a sign.  Neither a nor b is mean-zero here.
Both are log ratios to a TRAILING median, and a trailing median of a
persistent, right-skewed series is not the current median of it, so
the split is off-centre by construction rather than by accident.

Dropping the centring assumption costs nothing but an integral.  For
(a, b) bivariate normal with means mu and standard deviations sd, put
alpha = -mu_a/sd_a and beta = -mu_b/sd_b.  Then

    accuracy of 1{a>0} = 1 - Phi(alpha) - Phi(beta) + 2 Phi2(alpha, beta; rho)

which collapses to (*) when alpha = beta = 0, since Phi2(0,0;rho) is
1/4 + arcsin(rho)/(2 pi).  The BAYES rule among functions of a alone is
then a cut at c rather than at zero, and its accuracy is the same
expression maximised over c.  Three columns follow: what the centred
formula predicts, what the sign rule is really worth on a non-centred
split, and what the best cut on a is worth.
