================================================================================================
C.  THE HARD VERSION: TRAIN SHORT, TEST LONG
================================================================================================
Train on [0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21] only, then test at [34, 55] - delays longer than anything
the model has ever been fitted on. Interpolation is easy; this is not.

 delta  fitted for it  extrapolated   difference                  95% CI
    34         0.3863        0.3650      -0.0213   [ -0.05420, +0.00893]
    55         0.3736        0.3376      -0.0360   [ -0.08211, +0.00858]

  extrapolated delays not significantly worse than a purpose-fitted
  model: 2 of 2 [34, 55]

  The model learned how staleness behaves rather than memorising each
  case: trained on nothing beyond three weeks, it answers at eleven weeks
  as well as a model fitted there. That is the claim worth making for
  masked training, and it is the one this part was built to test.

================================================================================================
D.  HOW MUCH OF THAT LOSS IS THE PROTOCOL RATHER THAN THE METHOD?
================================================================================================
Part A cuts every training window at the LONGEST delay, so the pooled model
gives up 55 days of recent data that the per-delay model at delta = 0 keeps.
That is the right choice for one shared model and it is not free.  Rerunning
with each row cut at its own delay is NOT a legitimate protocol - short-delay
rows then see data a long-delay forecaster never had - but it brackets how
much of the gap is the cut and how much is pooling itself.

  cost of the conservative cut: +0.0051 to +0.0102 of R2

  Pooling itself, not the cut, is what costs.  Sharing one set of
  coefficients across delays is the binding constraint, and no change of
  protocol recovers it.
