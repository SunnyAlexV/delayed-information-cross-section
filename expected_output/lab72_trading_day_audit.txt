==================================================================================================
C.  HOW FAR ONE BAD ROW REACHES
==================================================================================================
  Yang-Zhang's overnight term is log(O_t / C_{t-1}), so a spurious row
  corrupts its own proxy value and the FOLLOWING day's.  The decision
  variable then divides by a trailing 252-day median, which carries the
  damage forward for a year of trading days.  The counts below are on
  dates the two versions SHARE - legitimate days whose values were wrong.

  series    dropped   proxy wrong   decision-var wrong   % of panel
  N225           30           148                  162       2.48%
  HSI             1             5                  144       2.18%

  A single wrong print is not a single wrong number.

==================================================================================================
  A weekend row is only evidence of an error when the cash index was

  series  date           previous close    this row   next close  error vs neighbours
  HSI     2008-11-30          13,888.24   17,572.00    14,108.84                 26%

  A row within ten per cent of its neighbours is misdated rather than
  invented, and is removed for being inadmissible, not for being wrong.

==================================================================================================
VERDICT
==================================================================================================
  Weekend-dated rows survive in 29 places after the gate, all of
  them in ['NSEI'], which is 29 rows and is intended.

  Identified as another instrument and removed: N225, HSI
  Identified as genuine weekend sessions and kept: NSEI

  The distinction matters in both directions.  Dropping every weekend
  row would have discarded 29 real NSE sessions - budget days, Muhurat
  trading and disaster-recovery drills - and keeping every weekend row
  left 30 Nikkei futures prints and one 26%-wrong Hang Seng print inside
  the estimator, where they corrupted a year of the decision variable
  each through the trailing median.

  What this changed in the paper: nothing it claims, and the last
  decimal of nearly everything.  The headline rate moves from 71.6% to
  71.5% and its interval from [54.4, 83.5] to [54.3, 83.4]; the
  identification statistic is unmoved at 5.40.  The multi-target tables
  of Sections S13 and S14 move by up to 1.3 points on Hong Kong, whose
  own series carried the bad print, and the counts those sections turn
  on (0 of 8, 1 of 24, 3 of 16, 8 of 16, read from lab51 and lab53) are all
  unchanged.

  The honest summary is that this defect cost the paper no conclusion
  and should still never have survived two years of checking.  It did so
  because every check here compared a timestamp with another timestamp,
  and a calendar is a different kind of fact.
