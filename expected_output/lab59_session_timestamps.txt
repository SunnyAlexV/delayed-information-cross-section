B.  DOES ANY DAY DISAGREE WITH THE RULE THE PANEL IMPLEMENTS?
============================================================================================
For every target, every peer and every date: the lag the fixed table
assigns against the lag the true closes imply. A LEAK is a day the table
calls same-day and the clock says the peer closed later or at the same
moment - the panel would carry a value the forecaster could not have had.
A waste is the reverse, and costs information rather than validity.

 target  peers   days   leaks  wasted  tightest margin      which peer is wasted
    SPX      7   7021       0    2754           3.50 h                 BVSP 2754
   FTSE      7   7021       0       0           5.50 h                         -
    DAX      7   7021       0       0           5.50 h                         -
   N225      7   7021       0    3345                -                 AXJO 3345
    HSI      7   7021       0       0           2.00 h                         -

  leaks across every target, peer and day: 0
  wasted same-day observations:            6099
  the narrowest margin the rule relies on:  2.00 hours (N225 before HSI)

  The waste is not symmetric and it is not noise. On 3345 of the
  7021 dates the fixed table sends AXJO back a day for a N225
  forecaster when the true clocks say it had already closed, because the
  table records both at the same standard-time hour and equality is
  resolved against admissibility. So the rule as implemented is STRICTER
  than the rule as stated: it discards information a real forecaster
  could lawfully have used. That direction matters for how the paper's
  numbers should be read. Every rate in this paper is what a forecaster
  achieves with less than the admissible cross-section, so the measured
  substitution rate is a lower bound on the rule's own terms.

  The rule survives real session clocks. Every peer the panel treats
  as same-day admissible did close before the target on every date in
  the sample, daylight saving included, and the margin it relies on is
  hours rather than minutes. Section 3's fixed table is a simplification
  of the clock and not an approximation of the rule.

============================================================================================
C.  HOLIDAYS: HOW OFTEN IS A PEER CARRIED FORWARD, AND HOW STALE?
============================================================================================
The panel is built on the target's calendar and peers are forward-filled
with a five-day limit, so a peer shut on day t carries its last close.
That is strictly older than t and cannot leak. What it can do is weaken
the instrument, so the frequency and the staleness are reported.

  the peer carried forward most often is N225 on 5.8% of the target's days
  A carried close is stale, never early, so this is a statement about
  the strength of the foreign block and not about its admissibility.
