================================================================================================
B.  THE INTERVAL
================================================================================================
Days are resampled in moving blocks; the curve, the combined model and the
benchmark variance are all recomputed on the SAME resampled days, and the
age is re-interpolated inside each replication.

   g   effective age          95% interval   at 0 days  off the grid
   0            4.6d       [2.8, 7.7] days           0             0
   1            6.8d      [4.4, 10.2] days           0             0
   5           12.6d      [9.7, 17.5] days           0             0

  'at 0 days' counts replications where the combined model beat a fresh
  mark outright; 'off the grid' counts those where it was worse than a
  55-day-old one. Both are reported rather than dropped.

C.  IS THE HEADLINE SAFE?
================================================================================================
  The headline quotes 4.6 days. The interval is [2.8, 7.7], 4.8 days wide.
  Relative to the 55-day mark being repaired, the interval spans
  5.1% to 13.9% of the original staleness.

g = 1 minus g = 0      2.1d       [1.4, 3.3] days         yes

================================================================================================
VERDICT
================================================================================================
  The effective age is 4.6 days with a 95% interval of [2.8, 7.7].
  Every value in that interval is a small fraction of the 55 days of
  staleness the cross-section is repairing, so the headline is a finding
  and not an artefact of reading one point off a curve. It should be
  quoted with the interval, which is what this lab exists to supply.

  A single day of foreign staleness costs 2.1 days of domestic
  freshness, interval [1.4, 3.3], which excludes zero. Section 9.3's
  exchange rate is a measured difference and not a direction, and it is
  now quotable in the unit the section is written in. The two marginal
  intervals in part B overlap, which is the ordinary behaviour of
  marginal intervals and is not evidence against a paired difference.
