C.  DO THE INTERVALS DEPEND ON THE BLOCK LENGTH?
================================================================================
Block length is a convention, not a derivation.  If the intervals move
materially across a factor of eight, they are an artefact of the choice.

 delta       dR2               block 5              block 10              block 21              block 42
     5   +0.0797     [+0.0520,+0.1055]     [+0.0498,+0.1098]     [+0.0489,+0.1087]     [+0.0492,+0.1087]

    21   +0.2495     [+0.2011,+0.2972]     [+0.1883,+0.3127]     [+0.1746,+0.3205]     [+0.1707,+0.3237]
    55   +0.3657     [+0.3160,+0.4141]     [+0.3004,+0.4286]     [+0.2836,+0.4414]     [+0.2707,+0.4550]

The point estimate does not move and all four intervals exclude zero, so
the CONCLUSION is robust.  The WIDTH is not, and it is not monotone in the
block either: width rises with the block at 2 of the 4 delays, and the
longest block (42) gives the widest interval at 2 of them.
The widest set by delay is d=5: block 10, d=13: block 21, d=21: block 42, d=55: block 42.
The paper quotes block 10; the conservative reading is the widest set at
each delay rather than any single block.

================================================================================
D.  IS THE RIDGE GRID BINDING?
================================================================================
Part B shows validation landing on an endpoint most of the time, which
looks alarming: a grid that is hit at its edge may be the thing choosing
the model.  The test is not where the selection lands but whether WIDENING
the grid moves the answer.  10-fold lower and 33-fold higher:

 delta   dR2 narrow   dR2 wide   difference
     0      -0.0099    -0.0107      -0.0008
     5      +0.0797    +0.0869      +0.0073
    21      +0.2495    +0.2537      +0.0042
    55      +0.3657    +0.3684      +0.0027

  narrow grid [0.3, 1.0, 3.0, 10.0, 30.0]
  wide grid   [0.03, 0.3, 1.0, 3.0, 10.0, 30.0, 100.0, 300.0, 1000.0]

The loss surface in the penalty is flat enough that the endpoint selection
is cosmetic: nothing moves past the third decimal.  The movement favours the
cross-sectional model at 3 of the 4 delays and runs against it at
1, the largest move in either direction being 0.0073.
The grid is reported because a reader should not have to take that on
trust, not because it is doing any work.
