================================================================================================
A.  WHY THE DAILY DESIGN CANNOT SIMPLY BE POINTED AT AN APPRAISED ASSET
================================================================================================
  the walk-forward needs 252 + 1250 + 250 = 1752 observations before its
  first forecast, and the proxy needs an open, high, low and close for
  every one of them.

     reporting frequency   observations per year   years to first forecast
                   daily                     252                         7
                  weekly                      52                        34
                 monthly                      12                       146
               quarterly                       4                       438

  A quarterly mark would need four centuries of history before this
  design made its first forecast, and would still have no intraday range
  to compute a proxy from. The design has to be re-specified at the
  frequency the asset reports. Whether that survives is the question.

================================================================================================
D.  WHAT A REAL STUDY WOULD NEED
================================================================================================
  At 109 test months the interval on R at a one-quarter delay is
  37% to 79%, a width of 41%.

  A bootstrap interval narrows roughly with the square root of the test
  window, so the months needed to reach a target width scale as the
  square of the ratio. On that arithmetic:

    target width    test months needed    years of marks
             60%                    51                21
             40%                   115                27
             30%                   205                34
             20%                   460                55

  That arithmetic assumes the interval narrows as it would on
  independent draws, which overstates how fast it really narrows on a
  series this persistent. The years column is therefore a floor and
  not an estimate.

================================================================================================
VERDICT
================================================================================================
  The experiment runs on monthly marks. At a one-quarter delay the rate
  is 52% with an interval of [37%, 79%].
  With the mark smoothed the way an appraisal is, 45% [31%, 76%].

  The frequency was not the obstruction. With no daily bars at all and
  109 test months, the rate at one quarter's delay excludes zero, and it
  still excludes zero when the mark is smoothed the way an appraisal is.
  The shape survives too: at one month the rate is indistinguishable
  from zero, exactly as it is at one day in Table 1, and it climbs from
  there. That is the daily result reproduced at a frequency twenty-one
  times coarser, on marks an appraised asset could actually produce.

  What the intervals will not do is price it. 41 to 46 points of
  width establishes that the effect is there and cannot say what it is
  worth, which is the right claim for a first study of an asset class
  nobody has measured. Part D says what history would narrow it.

  So the paper's own limitation should be restated. The illiquid case
  is not unreachable and it is not blocked by the absence of data. It
  needs a series with two decades of monthly marks and a public
  cross-section, run at the asset's own frequency rather than at this
  paper's, and this file is the rehearsal that shows the design holds
  when it gets there.
