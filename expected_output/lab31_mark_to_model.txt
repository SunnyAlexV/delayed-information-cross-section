================================================================================================
C.  WHAT DO THE EXTRA EXPOSURES COST?
================================================================================================
MARK uses one factor exposure, MARK7 uses seven. Section 5 says parameters
are expensive here; this prices them inside the reconstruction itself.

 delta      MARK     MARK7   difference                  95% CI
     0    0.5189    0.5189      +0.0000   [ +0.00000, +0.00000]
     3    0.4576    0.4603      -0.0027   [ -0.00539, +0.00054]
     5    0.4418    0.4471      -0.0053   [ -0.00955, -0.00033]
    13    0.3864    0.3972      -0.0108   [ -0.01905, -0.00137]
    21    0.3457    0.3523      -0.0066   [ -0.01630, +0.00448]
    55    0.2593    0.2655      -0.0062   [ -0.02032, +0.00767]

  delays where ONE exposure significantly beats seven: 0 of 6 
  delays where SEVEN significantly beat one: 2 of 6 [5, 13]
  (delta = 0 is identically zero in both arms: with no delay there is no
   shift to compute, so the reconstruction is the stale level unchanged.)

  This is the OPPOSITE of Section 5.1, and the difference is instructive.
  There, seven free coefficients were expensive because they sat inside the
  forecasting regression and had to be paid for out of forecast accuracy.
  Here they sit in a separate, much easier regression of changes on changes,
  are estimated from the whole training window, and are then used only to
  compute a shift.  Cheap parameters in the right place beat compression;
  expensive ones in the wrong place do not.  The lesson is about WHERE a
  parameter is estimated, not how many there are.

D.  DOES KEEPING THE DOMESTIC HISTORY HELP THE RECONSTRUCTION?
================================================================================================
MARK replaces the stale level with the reconstructed one and keeps the
weekly and monthly averages. MARK+ keeps the stale level too, so the model
can weigh the reconstruction against what it replaced.
