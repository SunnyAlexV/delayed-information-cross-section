VERDICT
============================================================================================
  Across the twelve conditional cells the nominal 95% set covers
  between 92.4% and 94.3%.  The cell the paper tells a
  practitioner to carry, stressed at eleven weeks, covers 93.1%.
  The same procedure on the whole sample covers 94.3%.

  So a conditional set is worth about 1.2 points less than the
  unconditional one, and that is a property of its LENGTH rather than
  of its identification: tau is comparable across the two, and the
  third of the sample is not.  Section 4.6 prints tau beside every
  set so a reader can see which are bounded; tau does not say what a
  bounded set covers, and this is the column that does.

  Reported so that a reader meeting a conditional interval is told
  what it is worth where they meet it, rather than being left to
  carry the full-window figure to a third of the sample.
