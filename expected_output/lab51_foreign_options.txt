D.  THE SPLIT IS BINARY; THE THING BEHIND IT IS NOT
================================================================================================
'Own options market' is a crude proxy for how close the available options
market is to the asset being forecast. That closeness is measurable, so
it can be used directly: correlate each target's decision variable with
the VIX block, and see whether what the cross-section still adds falls
away as the options market gets closer to the target.

  across the 8 targets, the correlation between how close the
  options market is and what the cross-section still adds given it:
  -0.37 on the levels, -0.33 on the ranks

  Eight points is not a regression and no interval is quoted for that
  number. What it does say is that the binary split above is not an
  arbitrary line through the panel: the closer the only options market a
  forecaster can see sits to the thing they hold, the less the foreign
  cross-section has left to give them, and the two groups are the ends of
  one gradient rather than two kinds of market.

================================================================================================
VERDICT
================================================================================================
  Redundancy is a property of holding options on YOUR OWN asset, not of
  options existing somewhere in the world.

  On the two targets with their own options market, implied volatility
  does not merely win, it wins by 31 points: 103% of the delay
  damage repaired against 73% for the cross-section, a rate above 100%
  being the paper's own signal that the options market supplies
  something the history never held. The foreign block adds
  significantly in 0 of 8 cells, which is Section 8's redundancy result
  reproduced on a second target.

  On the six where the options market belongs to another index that gap
  collapses to 3 points, 68% against 65%, with breadth ahead
  outright on 2 of the six. And the redundancy goes with it: the
  foreign block still adds given implied volatility in 1 of 24 cells, none of them at eleven weeks.

  Part D reads the split as a gradient rather than a wall, at -0.37 across
  the eight targets on the levels. It is a loose gradient and one
  target fits it worst: BVSP sits mid-range on closeness to the
  options market and still gives the cross-section no room at all.
  Eight targets cannot resolve why, and inventing a reason would be
  worse than leaving it where it is.

  That is the bridge Section 9 says the paper does not have. It is not
  an illiquid asset, and no equity index is. But the holder of one is
  in the second position and not the first: they can see an options
  market, they cannot buy one on what they own, and on the evidence
  here that is the configuration in which the cross-section keeps its
  value. Section 8's conclusion is narrower than it reads, and the
  case Section 1 opens with sits on the other side of the line.
