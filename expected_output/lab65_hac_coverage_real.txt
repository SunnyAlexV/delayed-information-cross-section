  retained share of the reference long-run variance: 85.6%
  which implies a coverage of 93.0% for a nominal 95% interval, by the
  arithmetic 2*Phi(1.96*sqrt(f)) - 1.  The fixed-b correction prices the ESTIMATION
  of the long-run variance and cannot price what the taper discards, so this
  shortfall survives it.  Any coverage figure simulated on a generator whose
  dependence is exhausted inside the bandwidth is measuring an easier problem,
  and belongs beside this number rather than on its own.

  Read the last column first: the reference has not converged, so neither has
  anything divided by it.  The delta = 3 column diverges outright, passing
  100% and climbing - a kernel cannot retain more than all of a variance, and
  a figure that does is telling you the denominator is broken rather than that
  the numerator is fine.


  worst nominal-95% interval in the table above: 93.4%

  'ret@b' is the share of the reference long-run variance a Bartlett
  kernel at bandwidth b retains.  'rule' is block_for(delta), the
  bandwidth the labs use at that delay.  If ret@rule is well under
  100% the standard errors are too small by its square root, every
  interval studentised by them is too narrow, and the Fieller sets
  inherit the same shortfall.  Note that ret@rule and a moving-block
  bootstrap at the same block share this limitation, so their agreement
  lab58 part 6 is not evidence that either is long enough: the two
  truncate the same dependence at the same lag.  Coverage is lab63's
  question, not this file's.

  The 40 column is kept because a flat 40 is what this project used
  before this measurement, so the table shows what the change was
  worth rather than merely asserting that it helped.
