0.  DOES THE ESTIMATOR RECOVER A KNOWN ANSWER?
==============================================================================================
For a bivariate normal, I = -1/2 log(1 - rho^2) exactly.  Draw samples of
the size this project actually has and see what the binned estimator returns,
raw and after subtracting its own permutation null.

A.  HOW FAR IS THE TARGET FROM GAUSSIAN?
==============================================================================================
The bracket N(Y)/Var(Y) is one for a Gaussian target and less than one
otherwise.  Smaller means the Gaussian formula UNDERSTATES how much room
a forecaster has.

  test days                      4862
  Var(Y)                         0.9085
  differential entropy h(Y)      1.3216 nats
  Gaussian entropy at same Var   1.3709 nats
  entropy power / variance       0.9061

  A target below one here: the ceiling that follows is looser than
  the Gaussian one by exactly that factor.

==============================================================================================
C.  THE SUBSTITUTION RATE WITHOUT A MODEL
==============================================================================================
The paper's rate is the ratio of what a fitted cross-sectional model
recovers to what the delay costs a fitted domestic one.  Both numerator and
denominator are properties of the estimator as much as of the information.
Here the same ratio is built from mutual information instead, using the
global factor of lab28 as the cross-sectional summary - so nothing is
fitted and no functional form is assumed.

  ceiling with a CURRENT domestic state (delta = 0): 52.5%

 delta    I(own)   I(factor)   ceil own  ceil factor   info R(d)   fitted R(d)      gap
     3    0.2380      0.2375      43.7%        43.7%       -0.6%         36.4%   -37.0%
     5    0.2004      0.2376      39.3%        43.7%       33.1%         51.4%   -18.3%
    13    0.1342      0.2375      30.7%        43.7%       59.5%         63.6%    -4.2%
    21    0.0886      0.2375      24.1%        43.7%       68.9%         67.3%    +1.7%
    55    0.0338      0.2375      15.3%        43.6%       76.2%         71.5%    +4.8%

  At the longest delay the two agree closely: 76.2% from
  information alone against 71.5% from the fitted models, a gap of
  +4.8%.  The paper's headline rate is therefore a property of
  the information and not of the estimator, which is the thing a referee
  cannot check from the fitted numbers alone.

  At short delay they diverge sharply - [3, 5] - and the sign is the
  informative part.  The information rate is LOWER there, which cannot mean
  the fitted model beat the bound: both models sit well below their own
  ceilings at short delay, so the cross-section can improve on a fitted
  domestic model without carrying information that model did not already
  have access to.  What the paper measures at short delay is partly the
  domestic estimator falling short of what its own data allows, and the
  information view separates the two for the first time.
